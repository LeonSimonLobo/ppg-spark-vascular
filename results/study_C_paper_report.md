## 2. Background and Motivation

### The Cross-Subject Generalisation Problem

A dominant concern in PPG-based blood pressure (BP) estimation is **within-subject data leakage**:
if the same patient appears in both training and test sets, models learn subject-level biases
rather than physiological features. Published benchmarks often report high accuracy under
within-subject or personalised protocols, but those numbers do not generalise to new patients
in clinical deployment.

Study C addresses this directly by:

1. **Strictly disjoint subject splits**: no patient appears in more than one cohort.
2. **Population-level (screening) framing**: the binary label SBP ≥ 120 mmHg tests whether
   a reading is elevated, avoiding the harder and noisier continuous regression task.
3. **Ablation ladder**: a stepwise feature ladder (C0 → C1 → C2m → C2) attributes gain to
   individual physiological signal sources.

### Why PPG Pulse Morphology?

The arterial pressure waveform shapes the peripheral photoplethysmogram through the mechanics
of pulse wave propagation. Arterial stiffening — a hallmark of hypertension — shortens the
systolic rise time and alters the dicrotic notch morphology. PPG features such as the systolic
rise time, pulse width, and decay ratio are thus candidate non-invasive proxies for vascular
stiffness and, indirectly, for elevated SBP.

## 5. Feature Engineering

### Raw Signal Processing

Each 10-second PPG segment (1,250 samples at 125 Hz) is processed by `src/features_c.py`
using the `extract_features_block` function, which implements a NumPy-based peak detection
and fiducial extraction pipeline (based on Elgendi et al., 2013):

1. **Beat segmentation:** systolic peaks detected by local-maxima search with refractory period.
2. **Foot detection:** inter-peak argmin (Amendment C2: unconstrained, replacing the prior 0.4s clip).
3. **Per-beat features:** rise time, pulse width, area ratio computed for each beat.
4. **Segment aggregation:** median across beats (robust to single-beat artefacts).

### Feature Catalogue

| Feature Name | Category | Physiological Meaning | Ladder Step |
|:---|:---|:---|:---:|
| `age` | Demographic | Patient age (years) | C0 |
| `sex` | Demographic | Biological sex (binary encoded) | C0 |
| `bmi` | Demographic | Body mass index (kg/m²) | C0 |
| `hr_ibi` | Heart Rate | Mean inter-beat interval derived heart rate (bpm) | C1 |
| `systolic_rise_time` | Pulse Morphology | Median foot-to-peak rise time (s) | C2m |
| `pulse_width` | Pulse Morphology | Median beat half-width at half-maximum (s) | C2m |
| `decay_ratio` | Pulse Morphology | Median area ratio (decay half / total) | C2m |
| `beat_count` | Signal Quality | Number of detected beats in segment | C2 |
| `snr_ratio` | Signal Quality | Signal-to-noise ratio of PPG segment | C2 |
| `ibi_sd` | Heart Rate Variability | Standard deviation of inter-beat intervals (s) | C2 |

## 6. Ladder Design

The ladder design enables fine-grained causal attribution of accuracy gain to physiological
signal sources:

```
C0: [age, sex, bmi]                                        — demographics baseline
  ↓ + hr_ibi
C1: [age, sex, bmi, hr_ibi]                                — + heart rate
  ↓ + systolic_rise_time, pulse_width, decay_ratio
C2m:[age, sex, bmi, hr_ibi, rise_time, width, decay_ratio] — + pulse morphology
  ↓ + beat_count, snr_ratio, ibi_sd
C2: [all 9 features + imputer flags]                       — full pipeline
```

Each step is trained independently (no feature cascading between models). Paired bootstrap
differences (C1−C0, C2m−C1, C2−C2m, C2−C0) quantify each component's marginal contribution.

## 8. Statistical Methodology

### 5-Fold Grouped Cross-Validation (Development)

- **Splitter:** `sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold(n_splits=5)`
- **Groups:** Subject IDs — no subject appears in both fold-train and fold-val
- **Seed:** 42 (applied to subject permutation before fold assignment)
- **Metric aggregation:** Out-of-fold (OOF) predictions pooled across all 5 folds
- **CI method:** 2,000 subject-clustered bootstrap resamples of OOF predictions, seed 42

### Held-Out Test Evaluation

- **Single pass:** Stage 1 of `src/test_c.py` scores all 70,212 AAMI_Cal segments exactly once
- **Bootstrap:** 2,000 paired subject resamples, seed 42, on per-segment predictions
- **Pairing:** For each bootstrap sample, the *same* subject IDs are used across both
  steps in the comparison (paired test), eliminating between-subject variance
- **Threshold:** Fixed at 0.5; no optimisation on test data

### Confidence Interval Interpretation

All 95% CIs are nonparametric bootstrap percentile intervals. Because resampling is clustered
at the subject level (not the segment level), the CI correctly accounts for within-subject
correlation of segments. A CI that excludes zero is interpreted as statistically significant
at the α = 0.05 level.

## 16. Limitations

1. **Binary classification, not regression.** The study predicts SBP ≥ 120 mmHg (elevated
   vs normal), not continuous SBP values. This is appropriate for population screening but
   does not provide individual BP estimates.

2. **ICU cohort.** VitalDB PulseDB is an ICU dataset. PPG signal quality, patient demographics,
   and physiology in ICU populations may differ from ambulatory or community-based subjects.

3. **No individual calibration.** All models are population-level (no per-patient calibration
   step). Personalised models with calibration data would likely outperform, but the
   calibration-free (CalFree) and AAMI_Cal conditions specifically evaluate the uncalibrated
   regime.

4. **Fixed threshold.** The decision threshold is fixed at 0.5. Threshold optimisation on
   a held-out validation set could improve balanced accuracy or sensitivity, but was
   excluded to avoid post-hoc tuning on test data.

5. **Aspirational benchmark goal.** The exploratory goal of +4 to +5 percentage points
   accuracy improvement was not used as a pass/fail criterion. The pre-registered endpoint
   is the point estimate and its CI.

6. **Single test evaluation.** The AAMI_Cal cohort was evaluated exactly once. No
   hyperparameter search, model selection, or feature engineering decisions were made
   after the test lock (`results/.test_locked_C`).
